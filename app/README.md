# Pilot quiz app

A static web app (plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework) that runs
the draft question bank for the pilots. Source in `app/src/`; the build
writes `app/dist/` (not committed).

```bash
python3 scripts/build_app.py          # -> app/dist/index.html (+ bank.js, app.js, style.css, config.js)
open app/dist/index.html              # works straight from the file, no server needed
python3 -m pytest -q tests/test_app.py   # clicks through the whole quiz in a headless browser
```

## Design

Built to look like a printed sample ballot rather than a web template:
white stock and black ink, square corners, ruled rows instead of card
grids, a black contest bar over each question, timing marks down the left
edge of each question box, and answers as ovals you fill in. Headings use
Archivo set condensed; body text uses Public Sans. A single highlighter
yellow marks you and magenta marks a friend you compare with. There's no
red or blue, so color never hints at a party. Light and dark themes share
the same tokens (`app/src/style.css`).

## What it does

- **Home page**: the headline, the length picker (Quick / Standard /
  Everything), a map of all the comparison groups (tap a dot to see who it
  is), and a live sample question that shows which way an answer moves you.
  A short before-you-start screen follows.
- **Answering**: tap an oval and the quiz moves on by itself (set
  `autoAdvance: false` in `config.js` to require Next). Back is always
  there. Every format in the bank is supported: position ladders, belief,
  frequency, defined agree/disagree scales (each point's meaning shown),
  tradeoffs (the two goals side by side, then one row of ovals from
  "Strongly" one side to "Strongly" the other) and exception follow-ups
  (a ballot-measure grid: answer names across the top, one row per case,
  with "Not sure"; stacked on phones). Defined words are dotted; inside an
  answer they're listed under the answers so the answer stays one button.
  Number keys pick answers (key hints show on mouse-and-keyboard devices).
- **Ways out**: "I don't know", "None of these fit" (with an optional text
  box) and "Skip" under every question, all scored as missing.
- **Answer direction**: forward or reversed once per person, for the whole
  quiz (recorded with each answer; codes never change), so scales don't flip
  between questions. Tradeoff sides are swapped the same way.
- **Order**: topic areas in random order per person; follow-ups right
  after their question and only when the earlier answer calls for them;
  the second half of each agree/disagree pair placed later in its topic.
  The header shows "12 of 107 · about 20 min left"; the progress bar has one
  segment per topic; the contest bar shows "Question 2 of 4" in the topic.
- **Quality**: three attention checks in the middle third, six repeated
  questions at the end (same direction as the first time), per-screen
  timing (capped at 5 minutes), and a last "how carefully did you answer"
  question.
- **Breaks** about every 12 minutes of active time, with an early look at
  your overall score and closest match.
- **Priorities**: one screen, tap up to five topics in order (stored as
  `pri.top`).
- **About you**: optional, after the questions; party lean and evangelical
  follow-ups appear only when relevant. Under-18 answers are never sent.
- **Save and resume**: progress is saved on the device after every answer.
- **Report a problem**: a link under each question for anything confusing
  or slanted; reports come through with the answers.

## Modes and results

- **Quick** (~25 min, the default): `items/modules/quick.json`, about 100
  questions covering every axis. **Standard** (~45 min): the link block plus
  3 of 9 balanced blocks. **Everything** (~2 hours): all 469.
- **Scores** are provisional, from hand-set keys in `items/keying.txt`: each
  answer runs from −1 (first option) to +1 (last), times the key's direction
  for each axis it touches; an axis shows once it has 3+ answers.
- **Balanced axes**: free speech and federalism weight left-coded and
  right-coded questions equally (tags in `items/modules/axes.json`,
  `balanced`), so they measure consistency rather than partisanship; the gap
  between the two halves shows on the axis card as even-handed or lopsided.
- **Comparison groups**: 84 profiles in six categories (US today, Pew voter
  types, presidents, US history, ideologies, abroad), generated by
  `scripts/revisions/rev_2026_10_13_profiles.py` and then
  `rev_2026_10_14_sim_profiles.py` into `items/modules/profiles.json`. Today's
  factions, the Bush-era GOP and the last five presidents are placed at the
  average simulated scores of their leading politicians (`sim/`; past
  presidents from record-in-office answer sets in `sim/records.py`); the rest
  are hand-placed. Match = 100 × (1 − RMS distance / 2) over the
  axes both sides have (overall counted double, at least 6 shared axes, plus
  a small prior so sparsely placed groups don't win too easily).
- **Results report**, in six tabs:
  - *Summary*: closest match, a label built from the economic and cultural
    summaries (same-side cells tiered: center-left / liberal / progressive,
    center-right / conservative / very conservative; a libertarian rule for
    strong economic plus personal-freedom views) plus up to two strong
    cross-cutting views ("anti-establishment communitarian"), a written profile for each family of axes using each
    axis's plain-language bands, a consistency meter (left/middle/right
    count over the left–right axes, labeled consistent, mostly consistent or
    cross-pressured), where you break from your side, views that cut across
    left and right, the closest group in each of the six categories, a
    second match weighted by your top issues, strongest views and "since last
    time".
  - *Matches*: every group ranked, filterable by category, each opening to a
    head-to-head on every shared axis (agree / close / differ / opposed).
  - *Map*: defaults to overall left–right × liberty vs. authority (the
    largest cross-cutting dimension in the simulation); any two axes or
    summaries (liberty/authority, establishment/populist, economic,
    cultural), eight presets,
    category filter, nearest groups on that map.
  - *Axes*: each axis opens to your band, the full five-band range, a strip
    of every group's position, the nearest groups, and the answers that moved
    it with a confidence note.
  - *Topics*: your lean and every answer on each of the 25 topics, with the
    axes each topic feeds.
  - *Answers*: your ranked issues, answer counts, copy/download.
  Shared links get the same report (minus Topics and Answers, which need the
  answers themselves).
- **Explore** (header link, no quiz needed): all 84 groups with a full
  position profile, plain-language reading of every position, most similar
  groups and your match; all 31 axes with the five bands, every group ranked
  along it, and the questions that measure it.
- **Site**: About page (how scoring works, every axis, every comparison
  group, privacy), light/dark toggle, keyboard shortcuts (number keys
  answer, ← back), self-hosted fonts (Archivo and Public Sans, OFL), favicon
  and social preview image.

## Deploying on Netlify

`netlify.toml` has everything: connect the GitHub repo in Netlify and deploy.
Netlify runs `scripts/build_app.py`, publishes `app/dist`, and deploys two
functions:

- `/api/submit` saves each finished quiz to Netlify Blobs (on by default:
  `submitUrl: "auto"` in `app/src/config.js`).
- `/api/export?key=…` downloads all responses as JSON lines. Set an
  `EXPORT_KEY` environment variable in Netlify first (Site configuration →
  Environment variables); add `&what=feedback` for results-page feedback.
  Then: `python3 scripts/pilot_responses.py responses.jsonl`.

Opened as a local file, the app keeps answers on the device.
Set `experiments: false` in `config.js` to turn off the built-in experiments
(random hiding of background lines, the "Torn" option for half of people).

## Data it records

Per answer: code (bank order), escape choice and text, time on
screen, on-screen position chosen, display direction, tradeoff side swap,
definitions opened. Per person: random id and code, bank version, mode,
assigned blocks, question order, attention checks, priorities, optional
about-you answers, self-reported care, date (no time of day). No names,
emails, IP addresses or browser details.
